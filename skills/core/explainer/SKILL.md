---
name: explainer
description: Single-file HTML page where figures and tables carry every fact. Use when asked to "make an HTML", explain or summarize something as a page or report, or give context on a task, system, change, plan, or PR.
---

`explainer → map the system → tell the hero story → write the 5 facts and the deep items → draw the napkin → inventory facts, cut the rest → a section per figure → turn toggles into multiples → the guess under the hero → words last → drawers and recall → cut to the caps, keeping what is never cut → shoot, self-check, reader-test → fact-check page and states until clean → open it`

The reader is an engineer who learns visually and wants to keep the idea for months. The first screen tells **one concrete case in 3 to 5 steps on one drawing**, the hero story, ending on the complete hero picture. Sections after it cover the rest of the pack as figures, tables carry the facts, and sentences only say what a figure cannot (why, judgment, the ask). Readers and judges found one case on one fixed drawing easier to get into and to remember, and one case alone left half the pack uncovered, so the page needs both.

**The 5 facts and the deep items carry retention.** Before any markup, write the five facts the reader must still know in a month. Each is the load state of a main-flow figure, meaning what it shows with no click and every drawer closed. Then write up to 6 deep items, the decision rules, whys, and procedures a reader gets wrong a month later (when to roll back and how, why there is no flag, which error string means which gate, the order of a choice). Each shows in a main-flow figure state at most one click away, with its key words printed as a figure label. Neither lives only in a drawer, a hover, or prose. The page ends with one recall of at most 12 items, the 5 fact blanks and then the whys.

Two more devices carry recall, and neither hides anything. **The guess sits under the hero, and it is optional** (a trace that keeps its toggles may hold one too). On the step before the asked one, the reader clicks the box where they think the case stops next, and the hero moves to that step and grades the pick. Step buttons and "Show me" always work, and any step change clears the verdict. **The napkin** ends the page. It is one picture of at most 7 labeled parts that holds the whole model, in the core's order and stroke. A part starts blank only when a fact blank shares its `data-n`, and the pair is one recall item. Every other part stays labeled.

**The budget is hard.** [budget.sh](budget.sh) `<page.html>` counts words at 1440x900 with every drawer closed. It exits 1 when a cap fails.

Start from [TEMPLATE.html](TEMPLATE.html). Its components work as they are: `herostory` (the first-screen story: `svg.board` with `data-on`, `data-hot`, `data-stop`, `data-say`, `data-from` marks, `.hs-caps` captions, built Back, 1..N and Next buttons, `#hero-N`, an optional `data-ask` guess, stacked frames with reduced motion), `core` (a scenario map, with `.wide` and `.tall` drawings), `multiples` (2 to 4 scenario panels of one drawing, `data-diff`, `data-off`, `.mkey`, `.mnote`), `data-ask` guesses on hero, core and trace, `data-ok-label` pass text, `guess` (drag a quantity), trace, stepper, tabs, field columns (`.multi`), diff, scaled timeline with blockers, `.tbl` tables, the recall (napkin, `.keep` fact blanks, `.deep` whys, one counter, red bar past 12), `data-fact` and `data-deep` checks, inline gloss `.gl` inside `data-gloss`, a `.legend` slot in every figure for the codes it prints, drawers, scrollspy, theme toggle. If one falls short for this page, override it in the page with a comment saying why.

## Steps

1. **Map the system around the subject.** The files the task hands you describe the change, not the system, so research past them. Use code search, the wiki, related tickets, and invoke `recall`. Trace the user's action end to end, naming who starts it, each service it passes by its real name, the core object it checks (with a real example value), and who reads the result. Bound: every system on the page has a name, a 3-word role, and a place in the map. A hop you could not trace is drawn as `unknown`.
2. **Tell the hero story.** Write the page's claim in one sentence of 20 words or fewer. Pick one concrete case from the source whose path proves it, with real names and values, usually the failure today and what changes it. Raft: one write through S1 commits, then S1 is cut off and its next write is rejected at the term check. A change: one gift-card checkout, rejected in staging, then passed by the promote. A practice: one bug's life, from the bug to the rule that blocks it. Storyboard it before markup, 5 rows or fewer, each with the step, what changes on the drawing, the hero box it names, and its pack line. The last row is the complete hero. Each row keeps the previous row's state, changes one or two things, and names a box only when the case reaches it. The actors and the object the case is about (with its real value) are drawn from step 1. Take every outcome from a line the source states, since a storyboard row is no place for a new claim, and mark an inferred step `inferred`. Build it as the template's `.herostory`, one `svg.board` about 360 wide with 14-unit text and every actor at a fixed place. Mark each stop with `data-stop="N"` and its real message. Step 1 shows on load and prints fact 1's key words, and each caption is 20 words or fewer. Bound: a reader who sees only the drawings in order, words hidden, can say at which step the case fails and what blocks it, the last step names every box, and budget.sh `herostory` passes.
3. **Write 5 facts plus up to 6 deep items before building.** A fact is one sentence of 14 words or fewer, traced to a source line that lives only in a `data-src` attribute on the element that shows it. Fact 1 is the hero's claim, and step 1 prints its key phrase. Pick the rest from what changes, what breaks, what blocks, how it runs, and what the reader must do. Name the figure whose load state will show each fact, and the one key phrase it must print as a label. That phrase becomes its blank. A deep item is a decision rule, a why, or a procedure from the source that a reader would get wrong in a month: the rollback when a label has moved, why there is no code flag, the error string per gate, a timing ladder, a failure sequence, the full order of a choice and its stop rule, a one-line test. Write each as a question with a 1 to 4 word answer, and name the figure state that shows it, its load state, one hero step or scenario other than the asked one, or one panel of a multiples figure. Bound: exactly 5 facts and no more than 6 deep items. Someone who knows only these can explain the subject to a teammate and avoid its traps, and no two share a job.
4. **Draw the napkin before writing any section.** Reduce the complete hero to at most 7 labels, counting boxes and arrow words together: the actors, the one or two things that decide, the part this change touches (`.hot`), and the outcome. Reuse fact blank words as part labels where they fit, and tie them with `data-n`. Use the napkin's names everywhere on the page. Bound: every section teaches one napkin part, one fact, or one deep item, and a section that does none is cut. The napkin keeps the hero's order and edges. Where the hero draws a sequence, the napkin draws the same sequence, never parallel branches, and a box the hero puts between two others stays between them. It uses the template's crisp stroke and label halo, no hand-drawn filter. Parts are numbered 1 to N in DOM order. The template prints a red bar otherwise, or past 7 parts.
5. **Inventory the facts, then delete most of them.** List every id, value, date, name, owner, count, command, rule, reason, and unknown in a scratch file, one per line. Mark each line that is a `decision`, `why`, `rollback`, or `trap` with that word. Then tag each `fact N`, `deep N`, `fig:<section>`, `drawer:<section>`, or `cut: <reason>`. A `drawer` line is reference the reader acts from (a command they run, a raw id they paste). Background, precedent, people, further reading, and source conflicts are `cut`, however true. A line marked `decision`, `why`, `rollback`, or `trap` may not be cut and may not go only to a drawer. It goes into a figure (a scenario, a label, a step) or becomes a deep item, and it keeps both halves, the rule and its reason or consequence ("persist votedFor before replying, or two leaders win one term"). A label that names the rule without its because or otherwise half does not count. When there are more than 6, fold two into one figure state. Prose is not a home. Bound: no untagged line, no fact has two homes, no marked line is cut, each marked line's figure prints its because or otherwise half, and the drawer lines will fit in half the main-flow words. When they will not, cut unmarked lines. Do not hide the overflow.
6. **Give each remaining question a figure, or delete it.** List the 2 to 5 things the reader still asks (why blocked, what can break, how it runs, what is open). Each becomes a section only when it can point to a figure that shows a mechanism a table cannot. Bound: every section names its figure and the line saying what changes or stops in it. A question with no such figure goes in a table under the nearest figure, or is cut. Pick the figure by shape.
   - Something gets rejected at a gate: a `trace`, one button per state (step 7 turns 2 to 4 of them into multiples), stopping red at the first gate that says no with its real message. Draw an inferred gate as `maybe` (hatched, with `?`), and say in the caption whether the request was proven to pass it. Use a trace only where some scenario really is rejected.
   - Blast radius: a bad change against a correct one, drawn as multiples (step 7), with a red mark on every output that fails.
   - Blockers: an arrow from each blocker into the step it gates, on the map, or a `.blk.bad` with a `.note` on the timeline lane it holds up.
   - An ordered flow where nothing rejects: a stepper, a verb on every edge. It loads on step 1, so it carries at most the fact step 1 shows.
   - Time: the scaled timeline. Mark go/no-go with `.gonogo` and contingencies with `.blk.cond`.
   - Before and after a one-line change: a `diff` under the figure it affects, not its own section.
   - Same fields per stage or option: tabs or small multiples. Rows of the same shape: a `clip` table.
7. **Turn toggles into multiples.** A section figure (never the hero, which keeps its steps and guess) that switches between 2 and 4 scenarios becomes a `.multiples`: one small copy of the same drawing per scenario, side by side on one scale, so the contrast shows in one glance instead of costing clicks. Keep the toggles when the order of the states matters (a stepper) or there are more than 4. Draw once in `svg.draw`, so every panel shares its viewBox. Mark the element that differs with `data-diff` (the accent) and what a case never reaches with `data-off`. Status stays a red or green mark plus the outcome's lead word. Each panel gets one `.outs` line, its outcome, copied from the toggle with its because clause and every scope word (`inferred`, `if X`, `normally`, `local test`). Use only the toggle's scenarios and outcomes, since a panel is no place for a new claim. Name the parts every panel shares once, in `.mkey`, and print a line true of every panel once, in `.mnote`, so panels repeat shapes, not words. A multiples takes no guess, since every answer is on screen, and a deep item names its panel with `data-via` at no click. Bound: budget.sh `multiples` passes (2 to 4 panels, one viewBox, an outcome and an accent in each, 4 labels or fewer a panel and 12 a figure, nothing cut off or overflowing at 390), each `toggles` warning left has its reason (order matters, or more than 4 states) in a comment, and every converted figure shows exactly the scenario set its toggle had.
8. **Put the guess under the hero, if it earns its place.** Add `data-ask="N"` to the hero and a `.ask` line. The question shows on step N-1 and asks where step N stops. The picks are `data-pick` marks already named by step N-1, and the answer is the pick with `data-stop="N"` (or `data-end`). A pick or Show me moves to step N and grades it, and any step change clears the verdict. A trace that keeps its toggles may hold the template's in-figure guess instead (`data-ask="<scenario>"`, GUESS badge, `desync` 0). A deep item never uses the asked step as its one click. Use `.guess` only when the size of a number is the point (how many files, how long until). Bound: ask only about mechanism (where it stops, which ones break, how many), never an id, date, or name. The guess must be answerable from the earlier steps and sections, and no caption or heading may name its answer. Never put a question block above a figure or hide a heading behind one.
9. **Write the words last.** The H1 is the claim, at most 2 lines. One lede of 2 lines or fewer follows it and grounds every term the claim needs with an inline gloss, `CheckoutService <span class="gl">(the service that takes payment)</span>`. Then the `.ask-line`, only if there is an ask. Then the hero story. No summary table on the first screen. Each section gets a claim heading and at most one caption sentence the figure cannot say. Expand an acronym, step code, or internal name at first use, or leave it out. In prose, wrap the term and its gloss in one element, `<span data-gloss>CHG <span class="gl">(change plan)</span></span>`. A figure that prints codes (`R1-3`, `P4`, `COD`) lists each in its `.legend`, one plain line per code. Call a commit "the code fix" and keep the hash in a drawer or a gloss. Hover (`.term`, SVG `<title>`) holds extras only, never a fact the claim depends on. Keep sentences under 20 words, active voice, real values, and mark claims that drive a decision `inferred` or `unknown`. A cause the source does not state is not written as "because".
10. **Fill the drawers, then the recall.** Only `drawer` lines go in `<details>`, under the figure they support, and a drawer holds what the reader pastes or runs, not explanation. Each summary says what is inside and how big ("Prod commands, 4 lines"). Then fill the recall under the napkin. `.keep` gets the 5 facts in page order, each `<li data-fact="N">` with one `.blank`, tied by `data-n` to the napkin part with the same label where one exists. Mark each fact's figure `data-fact="N"`. `ol.deep start="6"` gets only the deep items that ask what breaks without a rule ("Without randomized timeouts, what happens?" `split votes`), one `<li data-deep="<figure id>" data-via="<data-s>">` each with a `.q` of one line and its `.blank`. Its answer prints as a label at the box where the failure happens (9 of 12 exam questions are why or transfer), in the load state or after `data-via`. A procedure or rule that names no failure leaves the recall, and its figure still shows it. Bound: at most 12 recall items, drawers under half the rendered words, and no red bar (5 facts, each blank in its figure's load state, each deep answer at its failure box within one click, every `.q` asks why or what breaks).
11. **Cut to the caps.** Run [budget.sh](budget.sh). Every cap in the table is hard: `effective` 1000, `band` 180 in every 900px band, `recall` 12, `repeat` 0, `cite-leak` 0. No fact appears in two visible places, so a caption that repeats its figure's label or its section heading is cut first, and a readout that restates a step label goes next. Two counter facts decide where words cost most. `innerText` counts SVG text that a scenario or step hides at full weight, so a box label shared by two states is one `<text>` with `data-on="a b"`, not two. The band counter counts closed-drawer text at the drawer's place, so a drawer costs its full words in its band. Cut in this order until every cap passes: drawer text, then per-scenario readouts (cut words that restate a label, never the because clause, even when a box prints the same number), then captions, then table rows. Pairwise judges picked the uncut page 4 times of 4 when the trim took because clauses, so a cut that leaves an outcome without its reason is not a cut, it is a loss. Never cut a section, a figure, a hero step, a scenario or its panel, a deep item's failure label, or a fact blank. Three more things are never cut, because each cut last round made a page wrong or unreadable: a scope word (`in staging`, `normally`, `reliably`, `if X`, `local test`, a date or a stage), the first gloss of a term or code, and a figure's outcome state (its `settled:` end, its pass or fail mark, the test a gate applies, such as mechanical, universal, earned). A shortened line keeps its numbers, its `inferred` or `untested` mark, and both halves of a rule. Rewording a heading, the `<title>`, or a label writes a new claim, so check it against its source line before keeping it, and keep the `<title>` equal to the H1. A shorter H1 that drops rungs, a label that moves a verb to the wrong box ("State machine (it commits)"), and "every 2 h" for "normally every 2 h" each failed the fact check. Source names ("paper Figure 8", `raft.md:58`, "derived from") move to `data-src`. Bound: budget.sh exits 0 (`codes` included), the list of sections, figures, hero steps, and scenarios (buttons or panels) matches the list before cutting, and every fact on the page says what it said before, with the same scope.
12. **Shoot, self-check, reader-test.** Run [shoot.sh](shoot.sh) `<page.html> <out-dir>`. It writes 1440x900 slices as the reader lands (`first`), then light, dark, and all-drawers-open with every guess answered and the blanks filled, then every hero step at 1440 and 390 (`hero-N-1440.png`, `hero-N-390.png`) and the board with its words hidden (`hero-N-blind.png`), then runs budget.sh. In a browser, reload, close every drawer, paste [economy.js](economy.js), and confirm each fact is readable at its `y`. Click every control once, including each hero step, Back and Next, a reload on one `#hero-N`, one wrong guess, Show me, a step change after a guess, and each blank, and confirm the verdict agrees with the marks. Give a fresh subagent only the `hero-N-blind.png` crops in order. Its notes must name the step where the case fails and what blocks it, from shapes and color alone, and a miss means a shape or color change on the board, not more words. Bound: every slice has one dominant element, `desync` 0, no SVG text under 12px at 1440 or 390 (an SVG wider than 600 units gets a `.tall` twin about 340 wide), no overflow at 390 (every table in a `.tbl`), a green box shows its `data-ok-label`, no identifier wraps at 1440, and the page reads with JS off. Then give a fresh subagent a copy with every `<details>` and the KEEP section deleted. Ask for the claim, the 5 facts, each deep item's question and each step 6 question, what differs between the panels of each multiples and which panel fails, every guess made from what came before (flag coin flips or headings that give it away), and every term it could not explain. Fix each miss with a figure label or a gloss, never a new sentence, then re-run budget.sh. Bound: two thirds of the deep items right with no drawer, and budget.sh still exits 0.
13. **Fact-check the page and its states until both lists are empty.** Run [factcheck.sh](factcheck.sh) `<page.html> <source>` and `factcheck.sh <page>.states.txt <source>`, on a copy per pass so each pass keeps its list. The states file is how the checker sees each hero step's drawing. It runs two fresh models at once, the guard question and the truth judge's sweep (headings, then every label, outcome line and blank, then prose), both verbatim, and writes `<page>.facts.txt`, one item per line. Fix each item at its root (restore the scope word, mark it `inferred`, move the claim to the box it belongs to, or cut it), never by deleting a figure, a panel or a fact blank, and never by adding a hedge to a payoff line, then re-run budget.sh and factcheck.sh. Bound: both lists empty within 3 passes, and budget.sh still exits 0. After 3 passes, report each item left and why.
14. **Open it** with your platform's open command, and say which file it is.

## Look

- Whitespace separates sections. A section is a small number, a claim heading, a figure, one caption, then drawers. Nest no card inside another card.
- Spend color on status, and give each status a word too. The accent covers 10% of the screen or less.
- Use inline vanilla JS only, with no network requests, so the page works offline as one file. All content is in the HTML. JS only reveals or animates what is already there, and motion shows order or cause and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
